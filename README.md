# Mental Health in Tech: Global Survey Analysis (2017–2021)

## Problem Statement
What workplace and demographic factors most strongly predict whether tech employees 
seek mental health treatment — and what does this mean for how companies should 
design their support systems?

## Dataset
- Source: OSMI (Open Sourcing Mental Illness) Survey, compiled via Mendeley Data
- Period: 2017–2021
- Records: 1,242 responses from tech professionals across 40+ countries
- License: CC BY 4.0

## Tools Used
- Python (pandas, matplotlib, seaborn) — data cleaning & EDA
- MySQL — data storage & business queries
- Power BI — interactive dashboard

## Key Findings
1. 44.5% of tech workers report a mental health condition
2. When "Possibly" is included, 65% are affected or at risk
3. Workplace discussion culture — not age or gender — is the strongest predictor
4. Employees comfortable discussing MH with employers report conditions at 71% vs 29% for those who aren't
5. Companies offering mental health benefits have higher disclosure rates — not because benefits cause illness, but because they create psychological safety
6. The gap isn't willingness — workers score high on willingness to share (7-10/10) but lack institutional support

## Business Recommendations
1. Invest in psychological safety before investing in benefits
2. Train managers to initiate mental health conversations
3. Anonymous reporting channels reduce stigma and increase disclosure
4. Younger workers (Under 25) are underrepresented — targeted outreach needed

## Limitations
